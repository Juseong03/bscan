# Abstract

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are generated by back-splicing, a non-canonical splicing event in which flanking intronic sequences — particularly ALU/SINE repeat elements — facilitate junction formation through reverse-complementary base pairing. Despite growing interest in computational prediction of back-splice sites (BSS), existing deep learning models trained on internal benchmarks fail to generalize across genomic contexts, with performance dropping by up to 45% when evaluated on external datasets. We propose **BSCAN** (Back-Splice CircRNA Attention Network), a framework that integrates frozen RNA foundation model (FM) embeddings with a three-branch architecture comprising a 1D-CNN branch, a Watson-Crick stem map branch capturing intron complementarity, and a cross-attention branch encoding upper–lower junction interactions. Across four RNA FMs (RNA-FM, RNAErnie, RNABERT, RNA-MSM) and 10 transcript-grouped splits, BSCAN-FM attains the best internal AUC (0.879–0.885) using only ~2M trainable parameters; however, all models — BSCAN variants and baselines alike — fit the internal training transcripts almost perfectly (train AUC ≈ 1.0), so internal test ranking is overfitting-dominated and only weakly discriminative (the strongest baseline, CircDC, reaches 0.870 internally). The decisive evaluation is therefore cross-dataset generalization, where the picture is unambiguous: on an external circAtlas exon-aware benchmark, **only the FM models generalize** (external AUC 0.84–0.85; internal-to-external drop of just ~4–5%), whereas every one-hot and baseline model collapses (drop 16–45%) — most strikingly CircDC, which falls from the best internal non-FM score (0.870) to near chance externally (0.51). An architectural control (BSCAN-onehot, the identical three-branch architecture without FM) drops 33% to external AUC 0.57, confirming that the generalization advantage is attributable specifically to the frozen FM representation rather than to architecture; a branch ablation localizes the effect to the 1D-CNN branch (removing it inflates the drop to ~19–20%, while the stem and attention branches are near-neutral). To mechanistically assess what models have learned about intronic pairing, we introduce a three-tier hard-negative probing framework. CNN-based models discriminate ALU-rich back-splice introns from ALU-poor linear-splice introns (Tier 2 AUC up to 0.727), but no standard-trained model can distinguish individual back-splice introns (Tier 3 AUC 0.49–0.56). Hard-negative augmented training demonstrates that intron-pairing specificity is partially learnable from sequence data alone (one-hot models: Tier 3 AUC up to ~0.72), yet in the tested frozen-embedding setting, FM models remain near chance even under explicit hard-negative supervision (FM-hnaug Tier 3: ~0.50), suggesting that intron-specific signals are not readily recoverable by the downstream classifier in this configuration. Combination with ViennaRNA thermodynamic duplex energy provides a further orthogonal gain (+0.002–0.003 AUC; +0.011–0.017 AUPRC). Integrating cross-dataset generalization, motif safety, thermodynamic signal, and hard-negative robustness, BSCAN-RNA-FM+duplex achieves the highest composite biological profile score. These results establish BSCAN as the recommended framework for cross-context back-splice site prediction, while revealing a fundamental tension in FM-based RNA models between exon-bias resistance and intronic signal accessibility.
